# Reflection

One of the main challenges in this assignment was deciding the appropriate level of granularity for both state and activity diagrams. At first, there was a tendency to include too many detailed states and actions, which made the diagrams complex and difficult to read. For example, breaking down every small step in text processing or classification resulted in cluttered diagrams that were hard to follow. On the other hand, simplifying the diagrams too much risked losing important system behavior. Finding the right balance required focusing only on key states and actions that represent meaningful transitions, such as “Submitted,” “Processing,” and “Classified” for the news article lifecycle. This helped maintain clarity while still accurately representing the system.

Another challenge was aligning the diagrams with the Agile user stories created in earlier assignments. While the user stories and use cases described system functionality, they were sometimes named differently or grouped differently compared to the processes shown in the diagrams. For instance, the user story “Verify News” includes both preprocessing and classification steps, which are represented separately in activity diagrams. Ensuring consistency required carefully mapping each diagram to the relevant user stories and functional requirements without changing the original work. This process highlighted the importance of traceability, as it ensures that all system models are connected and reflect the same requirements.

Understanding the difference between state diagrams and activity diagrams was also an important part of this assignment. State diagrams focus on how a specific object changes over time by showing its states and transitions. In this project, the news article and classification result were good examples of objects whose states evolve from submission to storage and viewing. Activity diagrams, on the other hand, focus on the flow of actions or processes within the system. They show how tasks are performed step by step, including decision points and alternative flows, such as handling errors or invalid input.

Overall, state diagrams provide a clear view of system behavior at the object level, while activity diagrams provide a broader view of system workflows. Using both types of diagrams together improved the overall understanding of the system, as they complement each other by showing both “what changes” and “how things happen.” This assignment reinforced the importance of clarity, consistency, and traceability when modeling systems.
