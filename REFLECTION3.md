# Reflection

One of the main challenges in this assignment was prioritizing user stories and deciding what should be included in the first sprint. Since I am working alone, I had to take on multiple roles, including product owner and developer. This made it difficult to balance what is important for the user and what is realistic to implement within a short time.

Another challenge was breaking down requirements into smaller, manageable user stories. Some requirements were quite broad, so I had to think carefully about how to divide them into meaningful tasks that could be completed independently. Ensuring that each user story followed the INVEST principles also required extra effort, especially making them small and testable.

Estimating effort was also not easy. Without real development experience or a team to consult, assigning story points felt uncertain. I had to rely on my own judgment to estimate how complex each feature would be. This made me realize how important experience and collaboration are in Agile environments.

Sprint planning was interesting but challenging. Choosing which stories to include in the sprint required careful thinking about dependencies and priorities. I had to focus on building a minimum viable product, which meant leaving out some features even though they were important.

Overall, this assignment helped me understand how Agile development works in practice. It showed me that planning is not just about listing tasks, but about making decisions, prioritizing work, and adapting to constraints. Even though it was challenging, it gave me a better understanding of how real software projects are managed.
